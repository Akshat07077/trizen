Automotive sensors and electronic control units are the most technically sensitive category of automotive component packaging. ESD (electrostatic discharge) damage to automotive sensors and ECUs occurs at voltages as low as 30 volts — levels that are completely imperceptible to human touch but permanently damaging to microelectronics. The packaging material itself is the source of ESD risk when standard (insulating) plastics are used. Standard PP thermoforming generates and holds electrostatic charge at surface resistivity levels in the insulator range. ESD-safe materials dissipate this charge. This is not an optional feature for automotive sensor packaging — it is a fundamental requirement.

Pain Points We Solve

Three Problems Automotive Sensor Packaging Buyers Face

⚡
ESD Damage Creating Latent Field Failures in Automotive Electronics
Problem: Standard PP packaging generates and holds electrostatic charge at insulator surface resistivity levels. When a sensor or ECU contacts this packaging, the charge discharges through the component — causing permanent damage that passes visual inspection but causes field failure after vehicle delivery. This is the most expensive automotive failure mode — tracing field failures back to ESD damage at the packaging stage takes months and significant cost. Solution: ESD-safe PP and PETG packaging with controlled surface resistivity dissipates charge rather than allowing it to build up, eliminating the source of ESD damage before the component reaches the assembly line.
🔌
Connector and Terminal Damage During Transit Causing Assembly Failures
Problem: Automotive sensor connectors and ECU connector arrays are precision components — bent terminals, contaminated connector faces, or cracked connector housings cause immediate assembly failure that is traced to packaging damage. Solution: Cavity geometry with specific connector protection recesses — each connector terminal array is suspended in a clearance cavity that prevents packaging contact with connector faces or terminal pins. Connector geometry mapped from the component specification.
📡
ADAS Sensor Optical Surface Contamination
Problem: ADAS radar and camera sensors have optical surfaces — radar antenna arrays, camera lens housings — that must be free from fingerprints, particulate contamination, and micro-scratches. Standard packaging contact on optical surfaces causes performance degradation not detectable during vehicle assembly but manifesting as ADAS system calibration failure. Solution: ESD-safe PETG packaging with precision optical surface clearance cavity — no contact with optical surfaces. PETG crystal clarity also allows visual inspection of the sensor through the closed packaging.
Design Requirements

Sensor Packaging Design — Key Technical Requirements

⚡
ESD-Safe Material — Controlled Surface Resistivity
ESD-safe packaging materials have controlled surface resistivity in the dissipative range — typically 10⁶ to 10⁹ Ω/sq for ESD-safe PP, and similar for ESD-safe PETG. This range is chosen to dissipate charge slowly enough to prevent rapid discharge current through the component while still preventing charge accumulation. Surface resistivity is confirmed per material batch by CoA.
🔍
Cavity Precision — Sensor Terminals and Optical Surfaces Must Not Contact Packaging
Automotive sensors are precision devices. ADAS camera and radar sensors have optical surfaces requiring ≥1mm clearance from packaging contact. ECU connector arrays require terminal-clearance cavities. Cavity geometry is designed from the device technical drawing or physical sample — confirmed against dimensional specification before tooling.
🏷️
ESD Documentation — Supplier Qualification Requirement
Automotive electronics manufacturers require ESD-safe packaging suppliers to provide surface resistivity test certificates per material batch. Our ESD-safe PP and PETG is supplied with batch CoA including surface resistivity test data. Documentation available for automotive OEM and Tier 1 supplier qualification for ESD packaging programmes.
🔒
Individual Component Isolation — No Component-to-Component Contact
Multiple sensors in one tray must be individually isolated — no sensor-to-sensor contact during transit vibration. Each sensor in its own ESD-safe cavity, with cavity wall geometry that prevents component movement and inter-component contact. Particularly important for magnetically sensitive sensors where proximity to ferrous or magnetic components during transit could affect sensor calibration.
