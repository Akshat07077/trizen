Automotive exterior components present a specific packaging challenge that does not exist in most other industries: Class A surface requirements. A Class A automotive surface is a painted or coated exterior surface that is visible on the finished vehicle — any contact mark, scratch, pressure deformation, or chemical contamination from packaging contact is a cosmetic warranty rejection. The packaging must achieve zero contact damage, not just minimal contact damage. This requires specific material selection, cavity geometry design, and inter-leaf protection that standard thermoformed packaging does not provide.

Pain Points We Solve

Three Problems Exterior Component Packaging Buyers Face

🎨
Class A Surface Damage — Painted and Coated Surfaces Cannot Tolerate Contact Marks
Problem: Standard thermoformed trays contact the painted face of exterior components — leaving pressure marks, micro-scratches, or deformation that fails the Class A cosmetic inspection at OEM receipt. Solution: Cavity geometry designed to contact only the non-painted reverse face or edges — the Class A painted surface floats free of all packaging contact. Confirmed from paint zone marking on the component drawing or physical sample.
📚
Inter-Part Contact Damage When Exterior Components Are Stacked
Problem: Stacking multiple exterior components directly on each other — even with protective packaging — allows painted surfaces to touch during transit vibration, causing contact transfer damage. Solution: Foam inter-leaf protection between stacked exterior components as standard for all painted surface applications. Inter-leaf material confirmed from the surface coating specification.
📦
Profile Deformation Under Pallet Stack Load
Problem: Exterior trim panels, spoilers, and cladding components are geometrically thin and flexible — pallet stack loads cause permanent profile deformation if the packaging does not provide full profile support. Solution: Thermoformed cavity geometry contoured to the full profile of the exterior component — providing distributed support across the complete part geometry, not point or edge contact.
Design Requirements

Exterior Packaging Design — Key Requirements

🎨
Class A Zone Mapping — No Packaging Contact with Painted Surfaces
Before designing the cavity, the Class A zones (painted surfaces that must have zero contact) are mapped from the component drawing or confirmed from the physical part. The cavity is designed to contact only structural edges, mounting points, or reverse faces. Class A zone mapping is documented and confirmed with the customer before tooling.
🧱
Profile Geometry Support — Preventing Deformation Under Stack
Exterior automotive components are typically large, thin-walled parts. The packaging cavity must support the full profile geometry to prevent deformation under the stack loads of 10 to 20 high pallet stacking in transit. Support ribs, full-profile base support, and correct stack ledge geometry are designed from the component profile and the stack load specification.
🔗
Inter-Leaf Integration — Foam or Paper Between Stacked Parts
For applications where exterior components are stacked, foam inter-leaf or paper inter-leaf between parts is integrated into the packaging system. Inter-leaf material selection confirmed from the surface coating type — soft foam for painted surfaces, firmer foam for textured surfaces, paper or film for plated surfaces.
🧪
Material Compatibility — No Chemical Reaction with Automotive Coatings
Automotive exterior coatings are sensitive to certain plastic off-gassing and surface chemistry. PP is the standard material for exterior component packaging — it has negligible off-gassing and does not react with automotive paint systems. Material grade confirmed from the coating specification for premium or specialty coating applications.
