Automotive interior components present a packaging challenge distinct from exterior parts: surface finish variety. A single vehicle programme may include soft-touch foam-in-moulded surfaces, piano black gloss panels, leather-wrapped components, real wood veneer inserts, and textured hard plastics — each requiring a different packaging contact material and cavity design approach. A packaging system that works for a textured hard plastic door panel will damage a piano black gloss centre console trim. Interior fittings packaging must be specified per component type, not across a vehicle programme.

Pain Points We Solve

Three Problems Interior PackagingBuyers Face

🪞
Piano Black and Gloss Surface Damage from Standard Packaging Contact
Problem: Piano black gloss surfaces (centre console panels, dashboard inserts, pillar trim) show every fingerprint, micro-scratch, and contact pressure mark. Standard PP thermoformed packaging contact on gloss surfaces produces visible damage that fails cosmetic inspection. Solution: Packaging cavity designed to contact only the rear structural face, with the gloss surface facing away from all packaging contact. For applications where edge contact is unavoidable, a protective film liner is integrated into the cavity surface.
🧵
Soft-Touch and Leather Surface Compression and Contact Transfer
Problem: Soft-touch foam-in-moulded surfaces and leather-wrapped interior components compress permanently under point contact pressure. Contact transfer from packaging material leaves marks on soft surfaces that cannot be reversed. Solution: Cavity geometry distributes any structural contact over the maximum possible surface area — no point or edge contact on soft-touch surfaces. Material selection avoids plasticiser migration that can cause leather surface contamination.
🔌
Clip, Fastener, and Connector Damage During Transit
Problem: Interior trim components have clips, push-fasteners, and connectors on the reverse face that are damaged by packaging contact — causing installation failures at the assembly station. Solution: Cavity geometry includes protection recesses for every clip and fastener on the reverse face. Clip geometry is mapped from the component technical drawing before cavity design.
Design Requirements

Interior Fittings Packaging DesignKey Requirements

🎨
Surface Type Mapping — Different Surfaces Require Different Packaging Solutions
Before cavity design, all surface types on the component are mapped: soft-touch zones, gloss zones, fabric zones, leather zones, and textured hard-plastic zones. Each zone has a different packaging contact requirement. The cavity design document maps the packaging contact points against the surface type map before tooling is committed.
🔒
Clip and Fastener Geometry — Reverse Face Protection
Interior trim components are fastened by clips, push-studs, and wiring harness connectors on the reverse face. Every clip and fastener position is identified from the component drawing and provided with a corresponding protection recess in the packaging cavity. Damaged clips are installation failures — not cosmetic — and cause assembly line stoppages.
📦
Large-Format Geometry — Dashboard and Door Panel Size
Dashboard assemblies and door panels are large, complex components — often 800mm to 1,400mm in their longest dimension. Thermoformed packaging for large interior components requires full-profile geometry support to prevent deformation under stack loads and transit vibration. Our vacuum forming capability handles large-format interior component packaging from Vapi.
🧩
Multi-Component Interior Kit Organisation
Interior assembly kits — instrument cluster plus trim ring plus button sets, for example — arrive at the assembly station as an organised kit. The thermoformed kit tray holds each sub-component in its specific assembly position. Component positional organisation reduces assembly picking errors at the interior assembly station.
