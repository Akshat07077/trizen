Feeding bottle packaging has the most stringent material safety requirement of any baby care packaging application — feeding bottles and their accessories are in direct food contact, placing the packaging in the food supply chain. Thermoformed feeding bottle packaging must be certified to food contact material standards, confirming that the packaging material will not contaminate the food or liquid inside the bottle through packaging contact or proximity. Food-grade PP is the primary material for all feeding bottle packaging applications — it is certified for direct food contact, BPA-free, and chemical-resistant to the cleaning and sterilisation agents used for baby feeding equipment.

Pain Points We Solve

Three Problems Feeding Bottle Packaging Buyers Face

🍽️
Food Contact Certification — Feeding Bottles Require Food-Grade Packaging
Problem: Feeding bottles and their packaging are in the food supply chain — packaging materials that contact feeding bottles must be certified for food contact. Standard thermoforming materials are not food contact certified and cannot be used for feeding bottle packaging. Solution: Food-grade PP and food-grade APET with food contact certification — meeting IS/EU food contact material standards, with restricted additive lists and migration testing. Certification available per production batch.
🌿
BPA-Free for Infant Feeding Equipment Proximity
Problem: Baby feeding bottles and accessories are in proximity to infant food — any BPA migration from packaging to bottle to food to infant represents a health risk that baby brands and regulators have eliminated. BPA-free material specification is a minimum standard for all feeding bottle packaging. Solution: Food-grade BPA-free PP for all feeding bottle packaging. Material CoA confirming food-grade and BPA-free status per production batch.
🍼
Nipple and Teat Protection — Silicone Components Cannot Contact Standard Packaging
Problem: Silicone teats and nipples on baby feeding bottles must not contact packaging material surfaces — silicone surface damage or contamination from packaging contact affects both the feeding function and product safety. Solution: Cavity geometry designed to protect the teat or nipple from all packaging contact — teat suspended in a clearance cavity with no contact on the functional teat surface.
Design Requirements

Feeding Bottle Packaging — Key Design Requirements

🍽️
Food-Grade Material Confirmation — Mandatory Before Tooling
Food contact certification is confirmed before every feeding bottle packaging tooling commitment. Food-grade PP grade confirmed from the specific feeding bottle material (PP, PPSU, or glass) and the cleaning/sterilisation agent specification. Material CoA with food contact certification supplied per production batch.
🍼
Bottle Geometry Support — Stable Upright Orientation for Transit
Feeding bottle packaging trays designed for stable upright bottle orientation — bottles cannot tip under transit handling or pallet stack loads. Cavity geometry provides full bottle base support at the correct diameter, with neck support ring geometry that prevents bottle tipping without contacting the teat assembly.
🔒
Teat Clearance Cavity — Food-Grade Silicone Teat Protection
Silicone teat and nipple assemblies are protected by a dedicated clearance cavity in the tray design — no teat surface contact, no compression under pallet stack loads, no marking on the silicone teat surface. Teat cavity geometry confirmed from the specific teat assembly dimensions.
💧
Sterilisation Compatibility — PP Withstands Autoclave and Chemical Sterilisation
Baby feeding bottle packaging may be used during the sterilisation process in some supply chain formats. Food-grade PP has excellent chemical resistance to all standard baby bottle sterilisation agents — sodium hypochlorite, steam, and UV — and autoclave-compatible PP grades are available for sterilisation-phase packaging applications.
