Why Technically Demanding

Why Baby Care Thermoforming Packaging Is Technically Demanding

🌿
BPA-Free Is the Baseline — Not a Feature
Baby care packaging cannot use standard commercial thermoforming materials that may contain BPA (bisphenol A) or phthalate plasticisers. BPA and phthalates are endocrine disruptors with particular risk to infants. BPA-free material specification is not a marketing positioning for baby care packaging — it is the minimum material safety standard. All baby care thermoforming packaging from Trizen uses BPA-free PP and APET, with material CoA confirming BPA-free status per production batch.
🍽️
Food-Grade Certification for Products in Proximity to Food
Feeding bottles, sippy cups, weaning spoons, and baby food containers require food-grade thermoformed packaging — materials certified for food contact under IS/EU food contact material standards. Food-grade PP and food-grade APET have restricted additive lists and migration limits that standard thermoforming grades do not meet. Material grade confirmed from the specific baby product application at brief stage.
🔍
Crystal-Clear APET — Parents Must See the Product Through the Packaging
Baby product purchasing decisions are made at the retail shelf — parents inspect the product through the packaging before selecting. Packaging that obscures the product is rejected at retail. APET crystal-clear thermoforming provides full product visibility through the packaging — the standard for baby toy packaging, baby accessory packaging, and feeding bottle retail packaging.
🛡️
Tamper Evidence — Baby Product Safety Requirement
Baby products in retail channels require tamper-evident packaging — packaging that shows visible evidence of tampering before purchase. APET blister packs heat-sealed to branded paperboard card provide tamper evidence through the heat seal integrity — the seal cannot be separated and resealed without visible damage. Baby blister packaging is a must-rank keyword for Trizen's baby care packaging category.
